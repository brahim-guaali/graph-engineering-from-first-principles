$ python 06-reliability/arithmetic.py
1. Reliability decays exponentially with the number of uncertain decisions in a row:
     1 decisions at 99% each ->  99.0% of runs get through
     4 decisions at 99% each ->  96.1% of runs get through
    10 decisions at 99% each ->  90.4% of runs get through
    30 decisions at 99% each ->  74.0% of runs get through
    60 decisions at 99% each ->  54.7% of runs get through
   100 decisions at 99% each ->  36.6% of runs get through

2. A graph lowers the number of uncertain decisions. In our refund the step order, the
   $500 rule, the wait for approval and the payment are code. What's left for the model:
   loop:  ~7 choices (every tool call and the stop)  -> 93.2%
   graph:  2 judgments (classify, decide)            -> 98.0%

3. A narrow node with a sound check can retry. A judgment that is right 90% of the time:
   1 attempt  -> 90.0%  (fails 10.000% of the time)
   2 attempts -> 99.0%  (fails 1.000% of the time)
   3 attempts -> 99.9%  (fails 0.100% of the time)

4. And a narrow node reads less: our graph's decide reads ~550 tokens; the loop's last
   call read ~1,750. Model accuracy tends to drop as the input gets longer.
